03

Power distribution

One 12 V adapter feeds two things: the lock, directly through the relay contacts, and the HW‑688 buck. Everything digital hangs off the buck's 5 V output; the ESP32's own regulator makes the 3.3 V for the sensors.

04

Lock drive stage

The one part of the build where a wiring mistake costs you a board. Two details do all the work: the jumper comes off so the 3.3 V logic drives the optocoupler properly, and the pull‑up keeps the door shut through boot and reset.

Fail‑secure vs fail‑safe. A fail‑secure solenoid stays locked with no power and only draws current while the relay is closed — that is the wiring drawn above and the right default for a gym. A fail‑safe lock is the opposite: it is energised continuously to stay unlocked, drops open on power loss, and would keep 12 V flowing through your relay contacts 24/7. Check which one you bought before you power it up; if it is fail‑safe, invert the relay logic in firmware and size the adapter for continuous draw.

07

Before you power it up

Ordered roughly by how expensive the mistake is.

Kills hardwareSet the buck output before connecting anything

Wire the HW‑688 to 12 V with nothing on its output, meter the output terminals, adjust to 5.0 V, then power down and connect the loads. Some of these modules ship set near their input voltage — connecting the ESP32 first is a very short experiment.

Kills hardwareUSB and 12 V at the same time

On the DevKitC‑1 the USB VBUS and the 5V header pin sit on the same net with no ORing diode. While flashing over USB, disconnect the 12 V supply — or fit a Schottky (SS34) in the buck's 5 V line so the two sources cannot fight.

Resets mid-scanModem inrush

The 1000 µF at the A7670C VIN plus a 100 nF ceramic beside it is not optional in practice. Without it, an LTE transmit burst drags the shared 5 V rail down far enough to brown out the ESP32, and it shows up as random reboots that look like a firmware bug for about two days.

Opens the doorRelay state at boot

Fit the 10 kΩ pull‑up on GPIO40 and set the pin HIGH with pinMode/digitalWrite as the very first lines in setup(), before Wi‑Fi, display or anything else. Confirm on the bench that a reset does not click the relay.

Contact wearFlyback diode across the solenoid

Switching an inductive 12 V load with no diode arcs the relay contacts and radiates enough noise to corrupt the SPI display. A 1N4007 costs almost nothing and is the difference between a relay that lasts years and one that welds shut. Mount it at the lock, not at the relay.

WiringSeparate the lock cable from the signal cable

Run the 12 V lock pair in its own sleeve, away from the R503 and display ribbons. Keep the fingerprint cable under 30 cm if you can; if the sensor has to sit further out, drop the UART to 9600 baud rather than fighting the noise.

AntennaGive the A7670C room

Mount the LTE antenna outside the metal enclosure and keep it away from the buck's inductor and the solenoid wiring. Insert the SIM before power‑up — the module will not detect a hot‑swapped card.

Bring-up orderTest one module at a time

Buck → ESP32 alone → display → R503 → relay with no lock attached (listen for the click) → lock → modem last. Each step is a few minutes; debugging all seven at once is not.
